# A 4.4× Lighter Physical Warp Shell, and a Floor on How Light It Can Get

[![License: MIT](https://img.shields.io/badge/License-MIT-blue.svg)](LICENSE)

## Abstract

The only published warp-drive spacetime that satisfies all four classical energy conditions — the constant-velocity "warp shell" of Fuchs et al. (2024) — needs 2.365 Jupiter masses to carry passengers at 2% of light speed. Using the same radii and speed, we construct a shell of **0.54 Jupiter masses** that satisfies the null, weak, strong and dominant energy conditions (NEC, WEC, SEC, DEC) for every observer, by concentrating matter where the shift vector's momentum flux actually lives. A linear program over every possible shift profile then puts a leading-order floor of **2.7×10²⁶ kg** under this class of shells, so the "orders of magnitude" reduction hoped for in the original paper is not available here.

> **Status:** numerical result, independently cross-checked within this repository, **not yet peer reviewed** and not yet cross-checked in the authors' own Warp Factory toolkit. Review is very welcome — see [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md).

## Key results

All shells below have inner and outer areal radii of exactly 10 m and 20 m, as in the published benchmark, and all pass the full stress test described under [Validation](#validation--stress-tests).

| Shell | Passenger speed β | ADM mass | Jupiter masses | Compared with published | Tightest energy-condition margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fuchs et al. 2024 | 0.02 | 4.49×10²⁷ kg | 2.365 | — | not reported |
| **This work** | 0.02 | **1.02×10²⁷ kg** | **0.538** | **4.40× lighter** | +0.12% of local energy density |
| **This work** | 0.06 | 4.00×10²⁷ kg | 2.107 | **3× the speed at 0.89× the mass** | +0.21% of local energy density |
| Leading-order floor (any shift profile) | 0.02 | ≥ 2.72×10²⁶ kg | ≥ 0.143 | at most ~16× lighter is possible | — |

- **4.4× mass reduction at the published speed.** 1.02×10²⁷ kg instead of 4.49×10²⁷ kg, with all four energy conditions satisfied for all observers.
- **3× the speed for less mass.** At β = 0.06 the shell needs 4.00×10²⁷ kg, 11% less than the published shell at β = 0.02.
- **A floor on further optimisation.** To leading order in β and M/R, the Eulerian dominant energy condition (ρ ≥ |momentum density|) plus a spherical density give a minimum mass of 2.72×10²⁶ kg for these radii, scaling linearly with β. This is a *numerical leading-order bound*, not a formal proof — see [Limitations](#limitations).
- **Why the published shell is heavy.** The binding constraint is the null energy condition, which needs roughly ρ ≥ 2|q| along the momentum direction. The optimum concentrates matter into two thin layers at the shell's inner and outer surfaces, where the shift's momentum flux sits, instead of a near-uniform slab.

## Code & data catalog

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `warp_shell_minmass.py` | The complete pipeline and one-command reproduction script. Builds the exact Einstein tensor with SymPy (cached as a lambdified kernel), constructs shells from non-negative sources, checks the four energy conditions for all observers, computes the linear-programming lower bound, constructs the minimum-mass shell, runs the high-resolution stress test, and writes the outputs below. The `SLPShell` class is an experimental sequential-LP solver that did not converge and is not used by the default run. |
| `warp_shell_output/warp_shell_result.png` | Three panels for the β = 0.02 shell: (1) Eulerian energy density against twice the maximum momentum density across the shell, showing the density shaped to the momentum it carries; (2) the shift and lapse profiles; (3) verified mass against speed, with the published shell and the leading-order floor. | `warp_shell_output/warp_shell_results.json` | Machine-readable results: the lower bounds (any shift, and with the 5% buffer), and for each β the ADM mass (m, kg, Jupiter masses), the stress-test worst values for NEC/WEC/SEC/DEC, the tightest margin relative to local energy density, PASS/FAIL, areal radii, minimum lapse, compactness, and **every coefficient of every design** (density source, lapse source and shift spline coefficients, isotropic radii, shift support). ## Quick start

```bash
git clone https://github.com/IJai-code/warp-shell-minmass.git
cd warp-shell-minmass
pip install numpy scipy sympy matplotlib
python3 warp_shell_minmass.py                                  # β = 0.02 and 0.06
python3 warp_shell_minmass.py --beta 0.03 --extra-betas 0.05 0.08
```

The default run takes about 7 minutes on a 2-core Linux machine (416 s measured) and less on a multi-core desktop. The first run spends a few seconds building the SymPy kernel and caches it in `.warp_kernel_cache/`.

| Flag | Meaning | Default |
|---|---|---|
| `--beta` | passenger speed for the headline shell | 0.02 |
| `--extra-betas` | further speeds to construct and verify | 0.06 |
| `--buffer` | shift-free buffer at each shell edge, as a fraction of thickness | 0.05 |
| `--outdir` | output folder | `warp_shell_output` |

## Method in brief

1. **Metric (shell rest frame, static, G = c = 1):** ds² = −A(r)²dt² + P(r)⁴ δᵢⱼ (dxⁱ + Bⁱdt)(dxʲ + Bʲdt), with B = (−β S(r), 0, 0). S = 1 inside (flat passenger region moving at dx/dt = β) and S = 0 outside.
2. **Positivity by construction:** P = 1 + Φ with ∇²Φ = −2πs, and A = (1 − Ψ/2)/(1 + Ψ/2) with ∇²Ψ = −4πu, for non-negative B-spline sources s, u with ∫s dV = ∫u dV = M. This gives ³R ≥ 0, ∇²A ≥ 0, and an exactly isotropic-Schwarzschild (vacuum) exterior with ADM mass M.
3. **Exact stress tensor:** the full Einstein tensor for general A, P, B, projected onto the Eulerian orthonormal tetrad.
4. **Energy conditions for all observers:** NEC, WEC and SEC through their one-dimensional Lagrangian dual, so every admissible multiplier gives a certified lower bound at that point. DEC through min over n of [ρ + q·n − |q + Sn|], using 3,000 directions plus local refinement.
5. **Lower bound:** the weak-field momentum density j = (∇²B − ∇(∇·B))/16π is linear in the shift. Minimising ∫4πr² maxθ|j| dr over every shift profile is a sparse linear program, with every radial node constrained, including both edges.
6. **Constructive design:** the bound's optimal shift (edge kinks smoothed with 0.6 m cosine ramps, 5% shift-free buffers), then a linear program for the lightest non-negative density covering 2|q|. Density is raised locally wherever the exact check finds a violation, until none remain.

## Validation & stress tests

| Check | Compared against | Result |
|---|---|---|
| SymPy Einstein tensor (Alcubierre metric, comoving frame) | Alcubierre (1994), Eq. 19 | max relative error 1.5×10⁻¹⁵ |
| Static shell energy density | ρ = s/P⁵ (Hamiltonian constraint) | 6.5×10⁻¹⁶ |
| Interior and exterior | exact vacuum | 3×10⁻¹⁶ of peak |
| Exterior metric | isotropic Schwarzschild with mass M | agreement to ~10⁻¹⁴ |
| Einstein tensor of the final shell | independent 4th-order finite differences of the metric, no SymPy | 5.3×10⁻⁷ (truncation level) |
| Certified NEC/WEC/SEC bounds | multistart constrained optimiser (SLSQP) | 2×10⁻¹³ |
| Closed-form momentum density | full Einstein tensor as M → 0 | 5×10⁻⁹ |
| **Final shell, all observers** | 4,000 radii × two interleaved angular grids (91 + 90 angles) × 3,000 DEC directions, 48,001-point radial integration | **PASS**; worst value −8×10⁻¹⁷ of peak (roundoff at vacuum edges) |
| Final shell, tightest points | brute force: 20,000 null + 20,000 timelike observers and 1,500² null pairs at the 300 tightest points | all positive |
| Lower bound | radial resolution n = 200 / 400 / 800 | 0.1975 / 0.1978 / 0.1979 m (converged) |

### Claims withdrawn during development

Three intermediate claims failed later checks and were withdrawn. They are recorded here because they show what the checks are for.

1. **A 4.41× design** passed at 420 radii but failed at 2,000 radii (−8% of peak density) where the shift transition met the shell's outer edge. Fix: a shift-free buffer at both edges.
2. **A 3%-buffer design (3.94×)** passed at 2,000 radii but failed at 4,000 radii (−0.56%) at the inner end of the shift transition. Fix: 5% buffers, and every candidate must pass the 4,000-radius test.
3. **A first lower bound of 0.1255 m** left the two edge nodes of the linear program unconstrained, which let the optimiser hide momentum in edge kinks for free. Fix: constrain every node, with the shift continued flat beyond the shell. The corrected bound is 0.198 m.

The first two were sampling failures (violations between grid points); the third was a modelling error in the bound. [docs/FORMAL_VERIFICATION.md](docs/FORMAL_VERIFICATION.md) outlines how to rule out both kinds formally.

## Limitations

- **Still slower than light, still enormous.** Half a Jupiter mass to move a 10 m cabin at 2% of light speed. Faster-than-light travel without negative energy remains excluded by Olum's theorem (1998).
- **The floor is leading-order and numerical.** It is linear in β, neglects corrections of order M/R (about 4% here) and β², is computed on a discretised grid, and assumes a spherical density, as in the published shell. Non-spherical densities, or pressure support aligned with the momentum, could in principle go lower — down to roughly half the floor.
- **Pointwise certification, not continuum proof.** NEC, WEC and SEC are certified at every sampled point; DEC is sampled. Between points, confidence rests on resolution studies, which is exactly where two earlier designs failed.
- **Different matter model.** The published shell uses an isotropic TOV fluid; this shell has small anisotropic stresses (cancelled at linear order). Both satisfy the same four pointwise energy conditions. Neither addresses quantum inequalities, stability, or how the shell could be assembled or accelerated.
- **Same definition of transport.** As in the original, the warp effect is a uniform interior shift relative to the exterior. The original paper's light-travel-time test (that the shift cannot be removed by a coordinate change) was not repeated here.
- **Not peer reviewed.**
